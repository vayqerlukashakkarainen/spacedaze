# SpaceDaze Lore

## Core premise

SpaceDaze is the story of rebuilding **Drius Wake**, a destroyed pirate and
scavenger village, from the fragments that remain inside the **Phase Void**.

The Federation classified Drius Wake as an illegal salvage field. Under that
classification, its ships, buildings, machines, and inhabitants were declared
recoverable Federation property. The Federation dreadnought known as **the
Claimkeeper** attacked the village and attempted to erase it completely.

The village's damaged Phase Crown activated during the attack. It failed to
evacuate Drius Wake, but copied fragments of the settlement into the Phase
Void. Buildings, machines, memories, and the final moments of the attack now
exist there as unstable echoes.

The player's runs recover those echoes. Debris is therefore more than money:
it is physical matter carrying fragments of the Wake's phase pattern. Bringing
it home gives Burt and the Phase Crown enough material to reconstruct the
village.

The central narrative progression is:

> The Federation destroys the Wake -> the Void preserves its echoes -> Burt
> binds a pilot to Wake Station -> the pilot recovers debris and memories ->
> the village returns -> the restored Phase Crown reveals that the Wake
> survived -> the Federation returns to finish its claim.

## Drius Wake

Drius Wake, usually called **the Wake**, was an improvised orbital settlement
built from the wreckage of planet Drius. It became a home for independent
scavengers, pirates, deserters, unwanted droids, and ships that had nowhere
else to dock.

The Wake was disorderly but functional. Its residents repaired one another,
traded recovered machinery, and maintained their independence by reusing what
larger powers discarded. The Federation considered that independence both
criminal and economically inconvenient.

The hub seen by the player is the ruined core of Drius Wake. Its restoration
must feel like the return of a community rather than the upgrading of a menu.
Every lamp, facility, ship, and resident represents another piece of ordinary
life reclaimed from the attack.

## The Federation and the Claim

The Federation controls legal salvage, registered spacejump routes, and the
technology used to stabilize phase travel. Drius Wake violated all three. It
repaired unregistered ships, sheltered fugitives, and operated a Phase Crown
capable of guiding vessels without Federation navigation infrastructure.

The Federation called its attack a **reclamation action**. Survivors call it
**the Claim**.

The Claimkeeper is a Federation salvage dreadnought and the symbol of that
policy. Its purpose is not simply to destroy. It identifies, immobilizes, and
collects anything the Federation has declared valuable. What cannot be
collected is broken apart and pushed into the Phase Void.

Federation forces encountered during runs may be real patrols entering the
Void, phase echoes of the original assault, or a mixture of both. That
uncertainty should remain unsettling.

## The Phase Void and the Daze

The **Phase Void** is the unstable layer used by spacejump technology. Inside
it, location, memory, and physical matter can overlap. Scavengers call being
inside it **the Daze**.

The destroyed Wake survives in the Void as disconnected pockets. This is why
runs form changing rooms and why familiar structures, enemies, and events can
return in different arrangements. The Void is repeatedly reconstructing the
last patterns it received.

The deeper a pilot travels, the less stable and more hostile those patterns
become. Federation units phase-jump into recovered pockets when they detect an
active Wake signature.

Run upgrades are temporary phase configurations assembled from recovered
patterns. The Phase Crown cannot preserve every configuration when it pulls a
pilot home. Blueprints, knowledge, deposited debris, and restored hub systems
are stable enough to persist.

## Wake Station and the Phase Crown

**Wake Station** is the surviving technical heart of Drius Wake. It contains
the damaged **Phase Crown**, an array that once guided lost ships safely to the
village.

The Crown can hold a recorded phase pattern near the Wake. This is why the
player can be reconstructed after dying in the Void. Anything not secured to
the returning pattern, including loose carried debris, can be lost.

Each restored lamp stabilizes another portion of the Wake's pattern. When all
eight lamps are active, the Crown can once again transmit a navigational signal
powerful enough to guide lost ships home. That signal also tells the Federation
that Drius Wake survived.

## Burt

Burt is a highly skilled engineering and salvage droid. Before the Claim, he
kept incompatible ships flying by rebuilding components from scrap. He also
maintained parts of the Phase Crown.

Burt survived because he was gathering salvage outside the village when the
Claimkeeper attacked. He is angry at the Federation, frightened that it will
return, and ashamed that he survived when most of the Wake did not.

He expresses fear as anger and affection as maintenance. He rarely offers
comfort directly. Instead, he repairs something, gives an order, or insults a
pilot's technique. His confidence is strongest when discussing machinery and
weakest when discussing hope.

Burt transmitted an illegal phase snare after the attack because he needed a
pilot. That signal bent the player's failed spacejump toward the Daze. He saves
and reconstructs the player, but he is also responsible for stranding them.
This creates a relationship based on guilt, dependence, irritation, and
eventual trust.

Burt's long-term promise is to rebuild the Wake and repair the player's
spacejump drive. His private goal is to prove that the Federation did not
successfully erase his home.

## The player

The player begins as a pilot whose spacejump is pulled off course by Burt's
phase signal. Wake Station records the player's damaged phase pattern, binding
their reconstruction to the hub.

At first, the player enters the Void to survive and repair their drive. Over
time, that practical arrangement becomes a decision to help restore Drius Wake.
The player remains largely undefined so actions, upgrades, and relationships
can express their identity.

## Narrative progression

### Hub levels 1-2: Survive

The Wake is mostly dark. Burt repairs the player and explains the immediate
rules of the Void. The first systems and contracts come online. The player
learns that the ruins were once a village and that Burt deliberately bent
their failing jump toward his phase snare. Their alliance begins as an uneasy
bargain rather than immediate friendship.

### Hub levels 3-5: Remember

The salvage forge reconstructs more complex patterns. Signals and records from
the Claim begin to return. Residents remember what the Federation did. The
player begins to understand why Burt was desperate enough to strand them,
without being required to forgive him.

### Hub levels 6-7: Return

Docking gantries and traffic systems allow ships and surviving droids to return.
The Wake begins to feel inhabited again. Rebuilding is no longer a private
repair project; it is a public refusal to disappear.

### Hub level 8: Be seen

The Phase Crown awakens and broadcasts a route home. Drius Wake is restored
enough to announce its survival. The same signal reaches the Federation and
draws the Claimkeeper back.

## Supporting characters

- **Lamp Keeper** carries the emotional memory of the village. Its lamps mark
  restored districts and people who may once again find their way home.
- **Range Keeper** treats defense as procedure because admitting fear would be
  intolerable. Its obsession with calibration comes from failing to stop the
  Claim.
- **Ring Runner** represents the Wake's reckless independence. It would rather
  discover a dangerous new route than continue using paths chosen by the
  Federation.
- **Gloom and Jubilee** show ordinary, ridiculous village life returning. Their
  arguments matter because survival without personality is not restoration.

## Dialogue and tone

The story is melancholy, defiant, and funny. The destruction of the Wake is
treated seriously, but its survivors should not speak only in exposition or
grief. Their stubborn habits and petty disagreements demonstrate that the
village is alive.

Burt carries the main plot. Lamp Keeper provides emotional context. Other hub
residents should reveal one small piece of the Wake through their own concerns.
Ordinary conversations should remain short.

Canonical terminology:

- **Spacejump:** ordinary phase-travel technology.
- **Phase Void:** the unstable layer traversed during a spacejump.
- **The Daze:** scavenger slang for being inside the Phase Void.
- **Wake Station:** the surviving technical core of Drius Wake.
- **Phase Crown:** the Wake's reconstruction and navigation array.
- **Drius Wake / the Wake:** the destroyed settlement and the hub being rebuilt.
- **The Claim:** the Federation attack on Drius Wake.

Avoid introducing additional names for these concepts unless they represent a
genuinely different place, technology, or historical event.
